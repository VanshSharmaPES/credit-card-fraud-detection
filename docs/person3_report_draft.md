# Person 3 report draft: Results and Conclusion

Use this draft with the current result files. The comparison table reports test metrics at thresholds selected on validation. The raw amount analysis uses the project’s `Amount_raw` values and assumes a review fee of 5, 10, or 25 in the same units.

## Results

The held-out test split contains 56,746 transactions, including 95 frauds. XGBoost produced the strongest individual-model balance at its validation-selected threshold: precision 0.9740, recall 0.7895, F1 0.8721, and PR-AUC 0.8382. It generated two false alarms while detecting 75 of the 95 frauds. Among the five individual frameworks, fastai had the highest recall at 0.8000, detecting 76 frauds, with precision 0.8837 and F1 0.8398. TabNet had the highest individual ROC-AUC at 0.9764, while its PR-AUC was 0.6833 and its recall at the selected threshold was 0.7053. Because fraud is rare, PR-AUC, precision, recall, F1, and the confusion counts give more useful operational comparisons than accuracy alone.

The saved combined-model row has PR-AUC 0.8501, the highest value in the current table. That ensemble was built before fastai and TabNet predictions were available and uses XGBoost, Lightning, and Keras. Treat it as the earlier ensemble run, not as an updated combination of all five frameworks. Person 2 should rerun `08_ensemble` before the team makes a final claim about the combined model.

For the probability regression metrics requested by the project, XGBoost recorded RMSE 0.0203, MAE 0.0007, and R² 0.7529. The other saved models have negative R² values. These figures describe errors between probability estimates and binary labels; they do not replace the classification and ranking metrics used to assess fraud detection.

The money analysis treats every alert as a paid review and every missed fraud as a loss equal to its raw transaction amount. With a review fee of 10, the test split’s no-model fraud loss is 12,973.06 amount units. At the validation-selected F1 threshold, XGBoost detected 75 frauds with two false alarms, yielding total cost 3,685.70 and savings of 9,287.36. The cost-minimizing threshold selected on validation detected 70 frauds with the same two false alarms; on test it yielded cost 7,180.05 and savings 5,793.01. Fastai’s F1 and cost thresholds produced the same test decisions at this fee: 76 frauds detected, 10 false alarms, and savings of 9,626.34. The other models’ F1 and cost thresholds also produced the same test decisions at fee 10. The result shows that a threshold optimized for validation cost need not deliver lower cost on a separate test sample.

As the assumed review fee rises from 5 to 25, the validation-selected cost threshold generally becomes more selective because each alert is more expensive. This sensitivity analysis depends on the assumed fees and full prevention of a flagged fraud’s principal; it does not include customer friction, investigation time beyond the fixed fee, chargeback recovery, or other bank-specific costs. The test set is small in fraud count, so individual cases can move recall and cost materially.

Training and prediction times are those recorded in the result files and were measured on different CPU environments for the earlier models. They are evidence for this project run, not a controlled hardware benchmark. The fresh fastai and TabNet runs in this checkout used a CPU; their recorded training times are 6.02 and 45.55 seconds respectively, including fastai’s learning-rate search in its training time.

## Conclusion

For a workflow that places high value on limiting false alarms while retaining strong fraud detection, the current individual-model results support XGBoost: it detected 75 test frauds with two false alarms and achieved the highest individual F1 and precision. If the operating goal is to catch as many frauds as possible at the selected threshold, fastai detected one more test fraud than XGBoost, but produced eight more false alarms and had lower PR-AUC. The bank’s preferred trade-off between missed losses and review workload should determine the choice; the fee-10 test comparison also shows that validation-cost optimization did not outperform the F1 threshold for XGBoost on this holdout.

These findings apply to a stratified random split from a dataset covering two days of European card transactions in 2013. The PCA features V1–V28 do not have published business meanings, and the cost model uses assumed review fees and a simplified full-prevention assumption. The experiment does not establish performance on later transactions, other customer populations, changing fraud patterns, or a production review operation. A stronger follow-up would evaluate a later time period as a time-based holdout and replace the assumed review fees with estimates from the target bank. The combined-model conclusion should be revisited after Person 2 refreshes the ensemble using the new predictions.
